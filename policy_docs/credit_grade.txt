Credit Grade and Sub-Grade Policy

Loan applicants are assigned a credit grade (A through G) and a more granular 
sub-grade (e.g., A1, A2, B1) based on an internal risk assessment model. Grade A 
represents the lowest-risk applicants, while Grade G represents the highest-risk 
applicants. This grade is calculated using a combination of factors including 
credit score, credit history length, existing debt obligations, and repayment 
history.

Applicants with lower grades (D, E, F, G) are statistically more likely to default 
on their loan obligations based on historical repayment data. A lower credit 
grade is one of the most significant factors in determining loan approval, 
interest rate, and overall risk assessment. Applicants seeking to improve their 
credit grade for future applications should focus on timely bill payments, 
reducing existing debt, and maintaining a longer history of responsible credit use.

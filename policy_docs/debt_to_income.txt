Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio Policy

The debt-to-income ratio measures an applicant's total monthly debt payments as 
a percentage of their gross monthly income. A high DTI ratio indicates that a 
significant portion of an applicant's income is already committed to existing 
debt obligations, reducing their capacity to take on and repay additional debt.

Applicants with a DTI ratio above 35-40% are considered higher risk, as they have 
less financial flexibility to absorb unexpected expenses or income disruptions 
while maintaining loan payments. A lower DTI ratio, generally below 20%, is 
associated with a significantly lower likelihood of default and is viewed 
favorably in the underwriting process.
